# Product

## Register

product

## Users

World of Floorcraft serves ballroom dancers, teachers, competitors, studio organizers, competition staff, judges, scrutineers, and partners who need a reliable working reference for syllabus figures, routines, social context, and competition operations.

## Product Purpose

The product turns the ISTD ballroom syllabus and adjacent competition workflows into a precise, navigable, collaborative tool. Users should be able to inspect figure technique, move through graph relationships, build routines, communicate with dancers and organizations, manage registrations, run competition-day tasks, and review results without the interface becoming louder than the information.

## Brand Personality

The brand is editorial, disciplined, expert, and quiet. It should feel like an atelier reference manual: clear rules, sharp typography, exact materials, and enough ceremony to make the syllabus feel cared for. It is not a generic SaaS dashboard and not a sports event app painted with bright category colors.

## Anti-references

- Beige or brown shadcn stone defaults.
- Rounded card-heavy SaaS layouts.
- Decorative gradients, default glassmorphism, or neon dashboards.
- Purple-blue product palettes.
- Full-saturation status colors scattered across inactive UI.
- Display serif in tables, buttons, badges, form controls, list rows, or dense operational data.
- Side-stripe alert cards.

## Design Principles

- The metals carry meaning. Bronze, silver, and gold appear for syllabus levels, placements, membership, or earned status, not decoration.
- Neutrals do the work. The interface is mostly carbon, surface, chalk, mist, smoke, and rule lines.
- Serif is ceremony. Source Serif 4 belongs to brand marks, page titles, and major content headings. Inter carries body, controls, lists, tables, and operational UI.
- Square by default. Most panels, cards, inputs, menus, tables, and buttons use square corners or a 2px radius. Rounded shapes are reserved for avatars, toggles, progress dots, and other familiar circular controls.
- Flat by default. Depth comes from contrast, borders, bands, and rule lines. Blur is reserved for sticky chrome and modal/sheet overlays where it preserves context; menus stay opaque for readability.
- Dense but calm. Product screens should support repeated work and scanning. Prefer quiet structure over marketing composition.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Maintain high contrast in both dark and light modes, target WCAG AA for text and controls, preserve visible keyboard focus, and avoid relying on color alone for status. Motion should be short and state-based. Copy should be plain, specific, and readable for dancers and staff who may be using the product during real competition pressure.
